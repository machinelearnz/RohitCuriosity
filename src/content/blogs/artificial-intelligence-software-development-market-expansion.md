**𝗔𝗹𝗲𝘅 𝗜𝗺𝗮𝘀 – 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘄𝗶𝗹𝗹 𝗯𝗲 𝘀𝗰𝗮𝗿𝗰𝗲 ?** In his substack, economist Alex Imas argues that AI will drive down cost of production and economic share will move towards relational services. 

He argues that - Just as the Industrial Revolution mechanized agriculture, decoupling survival from intense human labor and allowing the rise of manufacturing and the service economy, AI is poised to trigger a similar structural reallocation. 

![Generated by Gemini](/images/img_1792306955502_preindeconomy.png)

Alex Imas argues that we are entering an era of hyper-abundance where AI will drive the marginal cost of producing digital and physical goods toward zero. 

This collapse in the cost of "stuff" does not lead to the end of work, but rather shifts economic value and scarcity to areas where human presence cannot be replicated. Consequently, the share of GDP dedicated to "human-intrinsic" services—the Relational Sector—will explode. In this new landscape, we stop paying for utilities and start paying for meaning, trust, care, and bespoke human interaction.

This article piqued my interest and I tried to evaluate Impact of AI on Software & Technology Services...

**𝗜𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗰𝘁 𝗼𝗻 𝗦𝗼𝗳𝘁𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗲 & 𝗧𝗲𝗰𝗵𝗻𝗼𝗹𝗼𝗴𝘆 𝗦𝗲𝗿𝘃𝗶𝗰𝗲𝘀**

In the realm of software and technology services, AI is exerting a similarly profound deflationary force on the cost of production. Just as tractors and industrial fertilizers exponentially multiplied agricultural yield per human hour, Generative AI and automated coding tools are dramatically increasing the productivity of software developers and engineers. 



![Generated by Gemini](/images/img_1792307046855_costofprod.png)


Tasks that once required weeks of bespoke engineering, such as writing boilerplate code, debugging, or generating synthetic datasets, can now be accomplished in minutes. This collapse in the "marginal cost" of creating software means that functional, high-quality code is rapidly becoming a super-abundant commodity.

**𝗠𝗮𝗿𝗸𝗲𝘁 𝗛𝘆𝗽𝗼𝘁𝗵𝗲𝘀𝗶𝘀: 𝗚𝗿𝗼𝘄𝘁𝗵 𝗧𝗵𝗿𝗼𝘂𝗴𝗵 𝗛𝘆𝗽𝗲𝗿-𝗔𝗰𝗰𝗲𝘀𝘀𝗶𝗯𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆**

However, there is a massive growth strategy hidden within this price collapse. As the cost of software creation drops, we can unlock a "Long Tail" of customers who were previously priced out. If custom enterprise-grade solutions become affordable for small & medium enterprises, the Total Addressable Market (TAM) doesn't shrink— **𝗶𝘁 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗼𝗱𝗲𝘀.**

**𝗖𝗼𝗻𝗰𝗹𝘂𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻**

My market hypothesis is built on the robust economic principle of the Jevons Paradox. This paradox, notes that as a resource becomes more efficient to produce (reducing its cost), the total consumption of that resource actually increases rather than decreases. 

Greater affordability will expand the Total Addressable Market (TAM). 

By dramatically lowering the "Utility Threshold" (the minimum price point at which a product's cost becomes low enough for a customer to justify its value and make a purchase decision), the industry can shift from selling expensive, one-size-fits-all tools to the few, to selling hyper-personalized, bespoke solutions to the many.
