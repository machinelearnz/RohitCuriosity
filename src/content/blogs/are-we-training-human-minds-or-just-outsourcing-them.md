A recent study in The Economist revealed a striking paradox in AI-driven learning: Researchers tracked 27,000 students using AI study tools.

- **Homework Scores**: Jumped 18%, while completion time dropped.

- **Exam Scores**: Dropped 20% below peers who did not rely on AI.



![Source : Economist](/images/img_1792304653156_aihomework.png)


The reason? Students who copy-pasted AI answers saved time but failed in unassisted tests. Meanwhile, students who used AI as an interactive tutor to unpack hard concepts maintained top performance.

**The Machine Learning Reality of Human Learning**

As an executive applying AI in capital markets and preparing for GARP’s Responsible AI exam, this research highlights a fundamental concept: **human learning functions just like training a neural network.**

Building real domain expertise requires cognitive friction—parameter tuning, repetition, and backpropagation in our own brains.

**When we use AI indiscriminately as a shortcut:**

- The **AI + Human Pair** performs well on surface tasks because the AI does the heavy lifting.

- The **Standalone Human Mind** remains under-trained. Without cognitive friction, the brain fails to form deep internal representations.

When stripped of external AI tools during high-stakes decisions, an under-trained mind gets lost—unable to reason from first principles.

**The Executive Takeaway**

This isn't just an educational issue; it's a critical risk for workforce development.

The goal for leaders isn't to ban AI, but to enforce Active Learning. We must encourage our teams to use AI to go deeper—stress-testing models, dissecting yield curves, or prototyping architectures—rather than using it to bypass thinking.

AI should accelerate the human mind, not replace the friction required to build genuine domain authority.

How are you ensuring your teams use AI to learn deeper, rather than just work faster?
