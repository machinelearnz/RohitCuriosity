For decades, treasury and risk desks faced a binary choice: **Buy** off-the-shelf vendor platforms and compromise on bespoke needs, or **Build** internally and absorb crushing engineering overhead, slow cycles, and maintenance bottlenecks.

**Generative AI has broken this deadlock.**

By closing the gap between domain expertise and executable software, AI empowers a new archetype: the domain expert who can independently conceptualise, architect, and deploy sophisticated analytical applications. 

This is not simply about writing code faster—it is about redistributing development capability across the enterprise. But democratising development also democratises risk. The real challenge is ensuring this agility remains auditable, secure, and institutionally resilient.

---

### 1. The Economic Shift: The Bilingual Architect

Traditional software procurement carries massive friction. Business teams draft 100-page requirement documents, hand them to developers lacking financial nuance, and wait months for systems that need immediate retrofitting.

Generative AI enables the **"Bilingual Architect"**—a practitioner fluent in both institutional finance and software design. When an SME writes requirements directly into functional code, prototyping cycles collapse from quarters to days.

As the marginal cost of code approaches zero, competitive advantage shifts from merely writing software to identifying core problems, architecting logic, and governing execution. 

This does not make core enterprise vendors obsolete. Transaction processing, clearing, and regulatory reporting still demand institutional scale. Instead, the paradigm is shifting to **Buy, Build, and Compose**:

* **Buy** foundational infrastructure requiring institutional scale.
* **Build** proprietary, high-alpha analytical modules.
* **Compose** interconnected workflows using APIs, open-source libraries, and AI tooling.

### 3. Historical Precedent: Spreadsheets and Shadow IT

Democratising computational power always accelerates innovation—and invites vulnerability.



![On the other hand ...](/images/img_1793714909372_yinyang2.jpg)


In the 1980s and 1990s, spreadsheets (VisiCalc, Lotus 1-2-3, Excel) bypassed IT mainframes and unlocked desk-level modelling. However, undocumented, unversioned macros birthed the crisis of **Shadow IT**, ultimately leading to catastrophic failures like the London Whale.

The industry did not ban spreadsheets. Instead, it instituted **End-User Computing (EUC)** policies and codified **Model Risk Management (MRM)** standards (from SR 11-7 to the recent SR 26-2). 

The lesson is enduring: technological democratisation does not eliminate governance—it makes it essential.

---

### 4. Governance in the Age of GenAI: The Responsible AI Imperative

When every analyst can build Python applications, launch RAG pipelines, or delegate tasks to autonomous agents, legacy IT approval queues collapse. Unchecked AI is simply automated operational risk.

Safe adoption requires institutional frameworks like the **GARP Risk and AI (RAI™)** curriculum, focused on four core pillars:

* **Transparency & Provenance:** Ensuring pricing engines and liquidity forecasts are reproducible, auditable, and mathematically explainable.
* **Tiered Model Risk:** Applying proportionate validation, conceptual soundness checks, and ongoing monitoring based on application materiality.
* **Data Sovereignty:** Protecting proprietary trade flows, counterparty exposures, and portfolio data within strict security perimeters.
* **Delegated Authority Controls:** Establishing rigid operational guardrails as workflows shift from passive information retrieval to agentic execution (triggering payments, modifying risk limits).

---

### 5. The Framework: The Sandbox and the Castle

To foster domain-led agility without compromising institutional safety, banks must adopt **"The Sandbox and the Castle"** operating model:



![Agility & Governance](/images/img_1793715777709_sandboxcastle.jpg)



* **The Sandbox (Agility & Experimentation):** A controlled, frictionless environment where domain experts leverage AI tools, explore open-source frameworks, and prototype solutions without six-month procurement cycles.
* **The Castle (Governance & Resilience):** The fortified production perimeter. Prototypes graduate to the Castle only after passing rigorous architecture reviews, data privacy checks, model validation, and operational testing.

These environments are strictly symbiotic. The Sandbox without the Castle produces operational fragility; the Castle without the Sandbox yields vendor stagnation.

---

### Conclusion: The Domain Expert as the New Architect

Generative AI's greatest impact is organisational: it dissolves the legacy wall between domain insight and technical execution. 

Competitive advantage will not belong to institutions with the largest IT departments or the heaviest vendor contracts. It will belong to those that empower business experts to build inside the Sandbox while anchoring their innovations to the Castle.

The question is no longer whether to Buy or Build. It is whether institutions can empower domain experts to innovate while preserving the governance that keeps financial systems sound.
